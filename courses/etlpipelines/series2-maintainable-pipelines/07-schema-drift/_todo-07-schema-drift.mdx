     ADDED 2026-09-02, and it closes the gap a working engineer hits most often. A coverage
     audit found ZERO mentions of schema drift anywhere in the academy, and it was not in the
     excludes either — the silence read as forgotten rather than decided.
     THE FAILURE IS THE MOST COMMON WAY A PIPELINE BREAKS OVERNIGHT, and none of it is the
     data's fault: the source added a column, renamed one, changed a type, reordered the
     CSV, or a vendor "improved" their export. Nothing in the rows is wrong. The SHAPE moved.
     IT SITS BEFORE 10 DELIBERATELY: shape, then values. This episode asks "does the source
     still look like what I agreed to read?"; 10 then asks "are the values in it sane?".
     Checking values first is how you get a green quality gate on a column that is no longer
     the column you think it is.
     WHAT IT OWES:
       DETECT      compare the incoming header or catalog against the shape you expect, at
                   the top of the run, and fail before a single row lands. Series 1 · 10
                   already does the spreadsheet version of this in one line; here it becomes
                   the discipline.
       CLASSIFY    not every drift is equal, and treating them alike is the mistake:
                     a NEW column        usually additive and safe to ignore — until it is
                                         the one carrying the money
                     a RENAMED column    silently becomes a missing column plus a new one
                     a RETYPED column    the worst, because it often still parses
                     a REORDERED column  harmless if you read by name, fatal by position
       RESPOND     fail loudly, quarantine, or absorb — and say which is right for each of
                   the four. "Absorb everything" is how a pipeline rots quietly.
     THE TRAP WORTH THE EPISODE: reading by POSITION rather than by NAME. It works for years
     and then a source inserts a column in the middle and every value shifts one to the left.
     Every row loads. Nothing errors. Every number is wrong.
     SEAM: Series 3 · 35 (data contracts) is the same idea pointed DOWNSTREAM — what your
     consumers may rely on. This is upstream: what you may rely on from your sources.
