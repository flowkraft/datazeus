    ── WHAT THIS LESSON SHOULD COVER (from curriculum.yaml) ────────────────
     COVER: costs, and the ordering rule that makes this episode land: MODEL THE COSTS BEFORE
     YOU LOOK AT THE RETURNS. Otherwise you have already decided the strategy is good and you
     will negotiate with the fee model.
     WHAT TO MODEL, from most to least certain:
       COMMISSION AND FEES        known, published, easy, and usually the smallest component.
       THE SPREAD                 you buy at the ask and sell at the bid. This is Series 1 · 05
                                  becoming a number, it applies to every trade, and it is the
                                  cost most amateur backtests omit entirely.
       SLIPPAGE                   the difference between the price you decided on and the price
                                  you got. Model it as a function of spread and of your size
                                  against typical volume, and be pessimistic.
       MARKET IMPACT              your own order moving the price. Ignorable at small size,
                                  which is a real reason a small account has an advantage —
                                  say so, and say where it stops being true.
       BORROW AND FINANCING       for short positions and anything held on margin.
     THE ARITHMETIC THAT ENDS MOST STRATEGIES, and it should be done on screen: cost per round
     trip multiplied by turnover. A strategy trading daily needs to clear costs daily, and a
     small edge multiplied by high turnover is a reliable way to pay a broker.
     THE RULE: A STRATEGY THAT ONLY WORKS AT ZERO COST DOES NOT WORK. Run every result at
     double your cost estimate and see what survives; if the conclusion changes, you did not
     have a conclusion.
