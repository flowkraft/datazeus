     THE PROBLEM, STATED SO IT CANNOT BE MISUNDERSTOOD: try enough strategies on one dataset
     and one of them will look excellent BY CHANCE. This is not a warning about carelessness,
     it is arithmetic — it happens to careful people, and it happens in proportion to how hard

       THE SUBTLE FORM THAT CATCHES EVERYBODY: you did not run a thousand backtests, you ran
       forty and looked at the results and adjusted. YOU ARE THE SEARCH ALGORITHM. Every
       decision you made after seeing a result spent some of your out-of-sample data.
     WHAT ACTUALLY HELPS, in decreasing order of usefulness:
       A REASON THE EDGE SHOULD EXIST, stated BEFORE testing — a structural argument about who
       is on the other side and why they accept the loss. A strategy with no story is a
       pattern in noise until proven otherwise.
       FEWER PARAMETERS, and a result that is FLAT across nearby parameter values. A peak that
       collapses when a window moves from 20 to 22 is a peak in noise.
       WALK-FORWARD, honestly done: fit on a window, test on the next, roll — and count every
       variant you ever tried, including the ones you abandoned.
       HELD-OUT DATA YOU TOUCH ONCE. Once. Looking twice makes it training data.
       ADJUSTING FOR THE NUMBER OF TRIALS — the deflated-Sharpe idea, which is the formal
       version of "you tried a hundred things, so a Sharpe of 1 means less than it looks".
