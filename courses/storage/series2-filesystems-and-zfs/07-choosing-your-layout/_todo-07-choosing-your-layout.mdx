     THE EPISODE THAT JUSTIFIES ZFS EXISTING, and the reason to teach it rather than any
     other filesystem.
     COVER: every block is checksummed, and because the filesystem also holds the redundancy
     it can DETECT corruption and REPAIR it from another copy. SILENT CORRUPTION — bit rot, a
     cable, a controller lying — is otherwise invisible: the disk returns bytes, they are the
     wrong bytes, and nothing tells you. A traditional filesystem on RAID cannot even tell
     which copy is right.
     SCRUB as the scheduled proof, and the practice: scrub regularly and read the output.
     THE HONEST FRAMING: this is the one property that would make a data team choose ZFS on
     purpose, and it is worth more to a data team than to almost anyone else — because a
     wrong number that looks right is exactly what this academy exists to prevent.
     ADDED after a review asked whether a reader could choose EXACTLY the redundancy they
     need — no more, no less. 05 teaches what a vdev is and states the mirrors-against-RAIDZ
     trade in two lines. That is not enough to lay out a pool you will live with for years,
     and the layout is the decision with the least room to change your mind.
     COVER, as a decision procedure rather than a table:
       THE LEVELS, IN ZFS's OWN NAMES   stripe (no redundancy), mirror, RAIDZ1, RAIDZ2,
                         RAIDZ3, and STRIPED MIRRORS — several mirror vdevs in one pool,
                         which is RAID 10's shape and the usual answer under a database.
       HOW MANY DISKS PER VDEV   a vdev is the failure and rebuild unit, so a twelve-disk
                         RAIDZ2 rebuilds by reading eleven disks and a pool of six mirrors
                         rebuilds by reading one. FEWER, WIDER VDEVS BUY CAPACITY; MORE,
                         NARROWER VDEVS BUY IOPS AND FAST RESILVER. Series 1 · 20's rebuild
                         window is why the second usually wins for a warehouse that must stay
                         up.
       THE RULE OF THUMB WORTH GIVING: mirrors or striped mirrors for anything a database
                         writes to; RAIDZ2 for bulk and archival; RAIDZ1 almost never at
                         modern disk sizes, for exactly the reason 1 · 15 gives about single
                         parity; RAIDZ3 when a rebuild takes days and you are honest about it.
     THE THREE DECISIONS YOU CANNOT TAKE BACK, and they are the point of the episode:
       ASHIFT   the sector size, set at pool creation and PERMANENT. Get it wrong on 4K-sector
                disks and every write is amplified for the life of the pool, with no symptom
                except that it is slower than it should be forever. Set it deliberately;
                do not let it be autodetected from a disk that lies about its geometry.
       VDEV TYPE   you cannot convert a RAIDZ2 into mirrors. Rebuilding the pool and restoring
                is the migration path.
       ADDING A SINGLE DISK   a bare disk added to a redundant pool becomes a vdev with no
                redundancy, and the pool now dies with it. ZFS will warn; people use -f.
     ⚠ RAIDZ EXPANSION LANDED IN RECENT OpenZFS AND CHANGES PART OF THIS. Check the version in

     packaged version actually supports rather than what the project supports.
     HOT SPARES and autoreplace, briefly, and the honest note that a spare shortens the window
