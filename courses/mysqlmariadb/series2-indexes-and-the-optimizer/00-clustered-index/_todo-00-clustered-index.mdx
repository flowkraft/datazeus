     THE CENTRAL FACT OF INNODB, and everything else in this series follows from it.
     COVER: the table IS the primary key index — rows are stored in PK order — and every
     SECONDARY index stores the PK as its pointer. Three consequences: a wide PK makes every
     secondary index wider; a random PK (UUIDv4) turns inserts into page splits all over the
     tree; and a query covered by a secondary index plus the PK needs no table lookup.
     This is why "just use a UUID primary key" is a decision with a cost, and where to say it.
