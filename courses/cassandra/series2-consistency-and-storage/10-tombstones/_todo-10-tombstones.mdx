     THE EPISODE THAT SAVES A CLUSTER, and the one most Cassandra teams learn the hard way.
     COVER: a delete writes a TOMBSTONE. It must be read by every subsequent query over that
     range until it is compacted away, and it cannot be compacted before gc_grace_seconds —
     ten days by default — because removing it early lets deleted data come back from a
     replica that never heard about the delete.
     WHAT THAT MEANS IN PRACTICE: a table you delete from regularly gets slower to read, the
     slowdown is invisible in the data model, and reads eventually fail on a tombstone
     threshold rather than on anything that looks like a cause.
     THE ANTIPATTERNS TO NAME OUTRIGHT: a QUEUE in Cassandra. A table where rows are removed
     after processing. A partition that is repeatedly rewritten. NULL INSERTS, which write
     tombstones without anybody typing DELETE — that one catches everybody.
     WHAT TO DO INSTEAD: TTLs so rows expire rather than being deleted, or time-bucketed
     tables you drop whole.
