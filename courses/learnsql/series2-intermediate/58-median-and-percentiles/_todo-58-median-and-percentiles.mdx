    ── COVER ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    THE ORDERED-SET AGGREGATES look unlike anything else in SQL. Present the shape, not a
    function signature:

        PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY amount)

      PERCENTILE_CONT   INTERPOLATES between the two middle rows
      PERCENTILE_DISC   RETURNS AN ACTUAL VALUE FROM THE DATA

    That is the episode's one real decision:
      DISC  when the answer must be a value that exists — a real price, a real duration
      CONT  for a continuous measure, where a number between two rows is meaningful
    A CONT median of 47.50 on a table where no order was ever 47.50 is correct, and will
    still get you asked whether the query is broken.

      MODE() WITHIN GROUP — the third member of the family, one line
      PER GROUP           — the whole thing under GROUP BY: a median per category, which
                            is where it earns its place in a reporting course
      p95 AND p99         — the same function with a different argument, which is how the
                            reader will meet it outside reporting
      NTILE, PERCENT_RANK, CUME_DIST — from the window family, with the distinction said
                            plainly: NTILE BUCKETS ROWS, a percentile RETURNS A VALUE.
                            People reach for NTILE expecting the second.

    ── PORTABILITY, IN THE LESSON RATHER THAN DEFERRED TO SERIES 4 ─────────

    MYSQL HAS NEITHER, and neither does SQLite. SHOW THE WORKAROUND: ROW_NUMBER() and
    COUNT(*) over the group, then take the middle row — or the two middle rows and average
    them when the count is even. It needs 2 · 35, it is genuinely fiddly, and a large share
    of readers will need it.
