     COVER: clustering, and the concept that decides whether it helps or hurts.

       REPLACES A NODE'S /etc/pve — so you join an empty node, and joining one with VMs on it

       COROSYNC AND QUORUM: a cluster needs more than half its votes to act. Which is why
       TWO NODES IS THE WORST NUMBER — either node failing leaves one vote out of two, no
       quorum, and Series 3 · 05's read-only /etc/pve on the survivor. The fixes: a third
       node, or a QDEVICE, which is a tiny vote-only daemon on any third machine — a NAS, a
       Raspberry Pi, the box in Series 4. This is the single most useful practical fact in
       the series for a small team.
       COROSYNC WANTS A LOW-LATENCY, RELIABLE NETWORK, ideally its own, and a redundant ring.
       A cluster whose heartbeat shares a link with a backup job will fence itself.
       REMOVING A NODE PROPERLY — a documented procedure, and one you cannot improvise.
     AND THE ALTERNATIVE FOR PEOPLE WITHOUT SHARED STORAGE: ZFS REPLICATION between nodes on
     a schedule. It is asynchronous, so a failover LOSES whatever changed since the last
     replication — one minute, five minutes, whatever you set. For many data workloads that
     is an acceptable and very cheap answer, and stating the RPO out loud is the whole skill.
