    ── WHAT THIS LESSON SHOULD COVER (from curriculum.yaml) ────────────────
     COVER: vzdump, and THE MOST IMPORTANT EPISODE IN THIS COURSE FOR A DATA READER.
       THE THREE MODES: STOP (shut the guest down — perfectly consistent, and an outage),
       SUSPEND (freeze it — shorter outage, older mechanism), SNAPSHOT (no outage, and the
       whole of the rest of this episode).
       COMPRESSION, bandwidth limits, and where the backup goes.
     AND NOW THE PART THAT MATTERS. A SNAPSHOT-MODE BACKUP OF A RUNNING DATABASE IS
     CRASH-CONSISTENT: it is exactly what you would have if you pulled the power cable at
     that instant. Restore it and Postgres replays its write-ahead log and comes up. Usually.
       "USUALLY" IS NOT A BACKUP POLICY, and the failure is not theoretical: with the disk
       cache mode from Series 1 · 25 set wrong, or with a device that lies about flushes, the
       snapshot can be missing writes the database believed were durable.
       THE QEMU GUEST AGENT is the partial answer: with it installed, Proxmox can ask the
       guest to FREEZE ITS FILESYSTEMS for the instant of the snapshot, which makes the image
       filesystem-consistent. INSTALL IT IN EVERY GUEST. It is also what gives you clean
       shutdowns and the guest's IP in the UI.
       AND THE REAL ANSWER, WHICH IS THE SENTENCE THE EPISODE EXISTS FOR: A VM BACKUP
       PROTECTS THE MACHINE. IT DOES NOT REPLACE A DATABASE BACKUP. You want both, and the
       database one — pg_dump, or PITR with a base backup and WAL archiving from Data Ops,
       Series 2 · 30 — is the one that gives you point-in-time recovery, that you can restore
       a single table from, and that you can restore onto a different machine entirely.
     THE POLICY TO LEAVE THEM WITH: VM backups nightly for the machine, database backups on
     the database's own schedule for the data, both off the host, and both restored on a
     schedule rather than on the day you need them.
