    ── WHAT THIS LESSON SHOULD COVER (from curriculum.yaml) ────────────────
     THE EPISODE THAT JOINS THIS SERIES TO THE OTHER TWO, and the reason TrueNAS is in this
     course at all.
     COVER: exporting storage TO the hypervisors.
       NFS FOR HYPERVISORS: a dataset exported and mounted as an SR in Series 2 · 10 or a
       storage in Series 3 · 10. Simple, thin-provisioned, easy to browse, and the disks are
       files you can see.
       iSCSI: the nouns — zvol, extent, target, portal, initiator — and what the hypervisor
       sees, which is a block device rather than a filesystem. Multipathing in a paragraph.
       WHICH TO CHOOSE, honestly: NFS is easier to operate and to debug; iSCSI is what some
       features expect. For most small estates NFS is the right answer and the course should
       say so rather than being coy.
     AND THEN THE FAMOUS TRAP, WHICH IS THE REASON THIS EPISODE IS `core`: SYNCHRONOUS WRITES.
     A hypervisor writing a guest's disk over NFS asks for its writes to be made durable. ZFS
     honours that, which means every write waits for the disk, which means guest performance
     falls off a cliff compared to local storage.
       THE WRONG FIX, WHICH IS EVERYWHERE ON THE INTERNET: set sync=disabled. It is enormously
       faster and it means a power failure on the storage box can lose writes the GUEST'S
       DATABASE BELIEVED WERE COMMITTED. That is the same violation as Series 1 · 25's
       cache=writeback, one layer further out, and it is worse because it is invisible.
       THE REAL FIX: a SLOG — a separate, fast, POWER-LOSS-PROTECTED device for the ZFS
       intent log. A consumer SSD without power-loss protection as a SLOG is a placebo that
       also loses data. Storage for Data, Series 2 · 20 has the mechanism; this is the
       deployment decision.
       AND THE THIRD ANSWER: decide honestly that this workload does not need it. Some do not.
       Saying that deliberately is different from disabling it because a forum said so.
