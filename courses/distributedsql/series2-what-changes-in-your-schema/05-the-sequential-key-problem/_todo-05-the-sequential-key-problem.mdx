     COVER: the specific failure that follows, and it is the same failure in three products,
     which is why it belongs in a concepts course rather than a product one.
     A SEQUENTIAL KEY — a serial, an auto-increment, a timestamp — means every insert sorts
     to the end, lands in the last range, and one machine takes the whole write load while
     the rest idle. The cluster is busy and slow and the dashboards look fine.
     THE SAME SHAPE, THREE NAMES: Cassandra's hot partition, MongoDB's monotonic shard key,
     this. A reader who sees the pattern stops making it in the fourth product too, and that
     transferability is the argument for teaching it here.
     THE FIXES AND THEIR COSTS: random keys spread and lose natural ordering; a hash-sharded
     index keeps the ordering and spreads the writes; a compound key with a varied leading
     part works when one exists. And the trade to state plainly — spreading the writes can
     scatter the range scans you wanted.
