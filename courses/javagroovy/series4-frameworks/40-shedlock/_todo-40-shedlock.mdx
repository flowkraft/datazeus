## What goes wrong

{/* BEAT 4 — the failure this prevents, and how you would notice it in real data.
    Every episode in this track earns its place by preventing a wrong number or a
    job that dies at 3 a.m. Name that here.

    lockAtMostFor IS A GUESS ABOUT YOUR JOB'S MAXIMUM RUNTIME, and both ways of guessing
    wrong are silent:
      TOO SHORT — the lock expires while the job is still running, the second instance
        starts anyway, and you get exactly the duplicate you installed this to prevent.
        This is the one that bites, because jobs get slower as data grows: the value that
        was safe in January is wrong by September, and nothing warns you.
      TOO LONG  — a job that died holding the lock blocks every later run until the window
        passes. A nightly job with a 24-hour window skips a whole day.
    lockAtLeastFor is the other half: it stops a job that finishes in milliseconds from
    being picked up again by a second node whose clock is slightly ahead — which is the
    README's clock-synchronisation caveat arriving as a real bug. */}
