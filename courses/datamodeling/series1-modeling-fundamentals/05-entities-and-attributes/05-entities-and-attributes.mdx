## Where does the second contact go?

The manager has a new request: for every customer, keep the **accounts contact** on file, not just the buyer.

The `"Customers"` table already has a contact. Look at it:

```sql file=./scripts/customer-contacts.sql

       "ContactTitle"
FROM "Customers"
ORDER BY "CompanyName"
```

## Listen: circle the nouns

Say the business in sentences again, and listen for the nouns:

- Northwind buys **products** from **suppliers**.
- **Customers** place **orders**, and a **shipper** delivers them.
- Every **product** sits in a **category**.
- An **employee** takes each **order**.
- Each customer has a **contact** — now, two.

Every thing a business keeps track of shows up first as a noun in a sentence. Product, supplier, customer, order, shipper, category, employee all have a table in Northwind. **Contact is the only noun without one.**

## Thing, or fact about a thing?

Does every noun get its own table, then? No. A noun is only a **candidate**. Three questions decide:

1. **Does it have facts of its own?**
2. **Can one owner have several of it?**
3. **Would anyone look it up on its own?**

A yes makes it a **thing** the business keeps track of, an **entity**, and an entity gets a table of its own. All no, and it is a **fact about a thing**, an **attribute**: a column in the table of the thing it describes.

Ten countries across 25 customers, and Germany alone appears 11 times. Does that make country a thing?

**Repeating is not the test.** In Northwind a country has no facts of its own, only its name. Nobody keeps a country's details anywhere. So today, country is **a fact about a customer**.

That is a decision, and it has a cost. The day finance needs a tax rate for each country, country has a fact of its own, and it becomes a thing with its own table. Nothing is wrong with deciding "fact" today, as long as you write the decision down and know what would change it.

## Two columns that travel together

Back to the contact. `"ContactName"` and `"ContactTitle"` always travel together, on `"Customers"`, and again on `"Suppliers"`. The same pair of columns in two tables is the classic disguise of a thing.

> **Two columns that always travel together are a thing hiding.**

Run the three questions on the contact, one at a time.

**Facts of its own?** Every contact has a title:

```sql file=./scripts/customer-contact-titles.sql
SELECT "ContactTitle",
       count(*) AS "Customers"
FROM "Customers"
GROUP BY "ContactTitle"
ORDER BY "Customers" DESC, "ContactTitle";
```

| ContactTitle         | Customers |
| -------------------- | --------- |
| Owner                | 6         |
| Sales Representative | 6         |
| Marketing Manager    | 5         |
| Accounting Manager   | 2         |
| Order Administrator  | 2         |
| Marketing Assistant  | 1         |
| Sales Agent          | 1         |
| Sales Associate      | 1         |
| Sales Manager        | 1         |

Nine different titles. A title belongs to the **person**, not to the company. That is a yes.

And suppliers have contacts as well:

```sql file=./scripts/supplier-contact-titles.sql
SELECT "ContactTitle",
       count(*) AS "Suppliers"
FROM "Suppliers"
GROUP BY "ContactTitle"
ORDER BY "Suppliers" DESC, "ContactTitle";
```

| ContactTitle         | Suppliers |
| -------------------- | --------- |
| Marketing Manager    | 2         |
| Order Administrator  | 2         |
| Purchasing Manager   | 1         |
| Sales Representative | 1         |

Six supplier contacts with four titles between them. One kind of thing lives in two tables, which is twice the reason to give it one home.

## The obvious fix: a second pair of columns

The fix nearly everyone reaches for first is to add a second pair: `"ContactName2"` and `"ContactTitle2"`.

![The Customers row for Alfreds Futterkiste with CompanyName, ContactName Maria Anders, ContactTitle Sales Representative, then two added columns ContactName2 and ContactTitle2 holding question marks, and a dashed ContactName3 labelled "a third contact?". Beside it, the question "Who handles accounts?" written as WHERE "ContactTitle" = 'Accounting Manager' OR "ContactTitle2" = 'Accounting Manager' OR one more for every copy, with the verdict: one condition per copy, one table change per new contact.](/images/data-academy/datamodeling-series1-05/contact-columns.svg)

Now ask the business's own question: **who handles accounts at each customer?** You would have to check `"ContactTitle"`, or `"ContactTitle2"`. A third contact means a third pair of columns, and a change to the table.

## Who handles accounts?

Today's model can only answer for the one contact it holds:

```sql file=./scripts/who-handles-accounts.sql
SELECT "CompanyName", "ContactName"
FROM "Customers"
WHERE "ContactTitle" = 'Accounting Manager'
ORDER BY "CompanyName";
```

| CompanyName       | ContactName     |
| ----------------- | --------------- |
| LILA-Supermercado | Carlos Gonzalez |
| QUICK-Stop        | Horst Kloss     |

Two customers. The other customers' accounts people are not missing from the query. **They are missing from the model**: there is nowhere to put them.

Numbered columns only move that wall. The pattern has a name, a **repeating group**: numbered copies of the same columns for something there can be several of. Every question needs one more condition for every copy, and the table changes every time the business grows.

> **Numbered columns are a thing there can be several of.**

## Give the contact a home

![Two boxes joined by a line labelled "has — one or more contacts". Customer holds customer id, company name and country. Contact, one row per person, holds name, role (buyer, accounts…) and which customer. Under them, two example rows: Alfreds Futterkiste, Maria Anders, buyer; Alfreds Futterkiste, to be named, accounts. Beside them: who handles accounts — one condition on role; a third contact — one more row; the table — never changes.](/images/data-academy/datamodeling-series1-05/contact-entity.svg)

Give the contact a home of its own: **one row per person**, with a name, a role, and which customer the person belongs to.

- The buyer and the accounts contact are just **two rows**.
- "Who handles accounts?" is **one condition on the role**.
- A third contact is **one more row**, not a new column, and the table never changes.

How one contact row points at its customer, and how to tell one contact from another, are the next lessons' subjects. For now, the decision is made: **the contact is a thing.**

## Three facts in one box

The last disguise hides inside a single value:

```sql file=./scripts/packed-quantities.sql
SELECT "ProductName", "QuantityPerUnit"
FROM "Products"
WHERE "ProductID" IN (1, 2, 12, 19)
ORDER BY "ProductID";
```

## When to unpack

Splitting every packed value is not the goal. Every extra column costs something to load and to keep right. Unpack a value only when:

- someone **filters** on it,
- someone **computes** with it, or
- a **rule** must check it.

If people only ever read it as a label, **leave it packed**.
